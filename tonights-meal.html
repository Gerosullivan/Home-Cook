🐟 Lemon & Thyme Butter Salmon with Crushed Baby Potatoes & Peas

~45 mins total
Serves 5
Salmon · Spring · Irish
🐟 Fishmonger pickup: Call in on the way home and ask for 5 salmon fillets, skin on, about 130–140g each. Spring salmon is in peak season right now — the fishmonger will be able to tell you what came in this morning. If salmon isn't available, fresh cod or hake fillets work just as well with this butter (pull them 2 mins earlier).

Ingredients

  • 5 salmon fillets, skin on (~130–140g each)
  • 800g baby potatoes, scrubbed
  • 250g frozen peas
  • 90g butter, softened (plus 15g for peas)
  • 2 lemons (1 zested & juiced, 1 sliced into thin rounds)
  • 1 small bunch fresh thyme (leaves picked)
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • Sea salt & freshly ground black pepper

Prep Ahead

~20 min prep
Before you leave for pickup at 5:00 PM — par-boil spuds, build the butter, assemble the salmon tray
Scrub the baby potatoes. Into a pot of salted cold water, bring to the boil, then simmer 10–12 mins until just tender (a knife slides in with a little resistance). Drain, tip onto a tray to steam-dry. before
Pick the leaves from the thyme. Zest 1 lemon and juice half of it. Slice the other lemon into thin rounds. Finely chop the garlic. before
Mash the softened butter in a bowl with the lemon zest, half the juice, the chopped garlic, most of the thyme leaves (save a few for garnish) and a good pinch of salt. Keep at room temp so it stays spoonable. before
Line a baking tray with parchment. Lay the salmon fillets skin-down, spaced apart. Dot a generous spoon of the lemon-thyme butter on each. Tuck lemon slices around the fillets. Cover with foil and put the tray in the fridge. before
Tip the steam-dried baby potatoes onto a second oiled tray. Leave uncovered — you'll crush & roast them later. before
While you're at pickup
Salmon sits in the fridge under its butter blanket — the flavours infuse and it stays safely cold. Potatoes dry out on the tray so they'll crisp up beautifully when crushed and roasted.
🧼 Food safety: Wash your hands, board and knife after handling the raw salmon. The tray goes into the fridge (not left on the counter) while you're away — the kitchen can warm up and fish doesn't forgive that.

Method

1
Preheat & start the potatoes ~15 mins
Crank the oven to 200°C fan. Drizzle the baby potatoes with the rapeseed oil, season with salt and pepper, and use the base of a glass or a fork to crush each one flat so you get craggy edges. Into the hot oven — they need a ~10 minute head start before the salmon joins them.
2
Salmon into the oven 12–14 mins
Take the salmon tray out of the fridge. Remove the foil. Slide it onto the rack above (or beside) the potatoes. Bake 12–14 minutes depending on thickness — the butter will melt and baste the fillets as they cook.
🌡️ Thermometer: Probe the thickest part of the biggest fillet. Pull at 50°C for pale pink and flaky — it'll carry to 52–54°C on the tray. Don't go past 58°C or the fish turns dry. Use one of your wireless probes — no guessing.
3
Cook the peas ~4 mins
In the last few minutes of the salmon cook, tip the peas into a small pot of boiling salted water. 3 minutes is plenty. Drain, return to the pot, toss with 15g butter and a pinch of salt. Lid on.
4
Rest & plate up ~2 mins
Pull the salmon out and let it sit for 2 minutes — this finishes the cook. Spoon some crushed potatoes, a generous scoop of buttery peas, and a fillet onto each plate. Spoon the melted lemon-thyme butter from the salmon tray over the top. Scatter the reserved thyme leaves.
👧 Kid-friendly notes: For Child 2 & 3 (the younger two), flake the salmon off the skin into small pieces and stir a spoon of the lemon-thyme butter through their peas — they'll eat more of each that way. The flavours are mild, buttery and non-spicy, which is exactly what they like. The crushed potatoes are basically roast potato edges — always a winner.
