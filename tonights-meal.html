🍏 Irish Pork, Apple & Thyme Skillet with Colcannon Mash & Buttered Peas

~55 mins total
Serves 5
Pork · Irish · Freezer-first
🧊 Freezer-first: Tonight leans on what's already in the house — pork mince from the freezer, savoy cabbage from the freezer, baby potatoes from the pantry, a couple of apples, frozen peas and a knob of butter. No shopping run needed. Pull the pork mince out of the freezer onto a plate in the fridge when you read this — it needs a head start to defrost.

Ingredients

  • Pork & apple skillet
  • 500g pork mince, defrosted
  • 1 large white onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 2 eating apples, cored & coarsely grated
  • 1 tsp dried thyme (or a few sprigs fresh)
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 250ml chicken stock
  • 1 tsp white wine vinegar
  • 1 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • Colcannon mash
  • 900g baby potatoes, skins on
  • 200g savoy cabbage (frozen is grand)
  • 80g butter (plus 20g for finishing)
  • 100ml whole milk, warm
  • Buttered peas & to finish
  • 250g frozen peas
  • 15g butter
  • Sea salt & freshly ground black pepper

Prep Ahead

~25 min prep
Before you leave for pickup at 5:00 PM — build the pork base, par-boil the potatoes, get the cabbage ready
Chop the onion and garlic. Core and coarsely grate the apples (skins on is fine). before
Heat the oil in a wide heavy pan over medium-high. Brown the pork mince in two batches, breaking it up until it's got real colour — 6–8 minutes total. Transfer to a bowl. before
Drop the heat to medium. Soften the onion in the pork fat, 5 minutes. Add garlic and thyme, 30 seconds. Stir in the grated apple, Worcestershire, vinegar and stock. Return the pork, season, simmer gently 10 minutes until most liquid is gone. Taste, adjust. Lid on, off the heat. before
Scrub the baby potatoes. Halve any larger ones. Into a big pot of well-salted cold water, bring to the boil, then simmer 15–18 minutes until a knife slides straight through. Drain, return to the pot, lid on, off the heat — they'll stay warm enough. before
Tip the savoy cabbage into a colander in the sink so it starts to thaw. Measure out 80g butter for the mash and pour 100ml milk into a mug ready to warm later. before
While you're at pickup
The pork and apple sit quietly in the pan soaking up each other's flavour — it actually tastes better after a 30-minute rest. Potatoes stay warm under the lid. Cabbage defrosts slowly in the sink so it's ready to wilt in seconds when you're back.
🧼 Food safety: Wash your hands, board and knife after handling the raw pork. Once the mince is browned and in the sauce it's safe to leave covered at room temp for 30–40 minutes. If you're gone longer, slide the pan into the fridge and give it a proper reheat when you're back.

Method

1
Reheat the pork & apple ~6 mins
Back from pickup — set the pork pan on medium heat with the lid on to come back up to a gentle bubble. Stir once or twice. If it looks dry, splash in 2 tbsp water or extra stock.
2
Wilt the cabbage ~3 mins
Squeeze excess water from the thawed savoy cabbage. Melt a knob of the mash butter (about 20g) in a small pan, add the cabbage with a pinch of salt and toss over medium heat until it's hot and glossy — about 2–3 minutes. Off the heat.
3
Mash the spuds ~5 mins
Warm the milk in the microwave 30 seconds. Back on a low hob, mash the potatoes (skins on, rustic is fine) with the remaining 60g butter, working it in until glossy. Loosen with the warm milk, bit by bit, until you like the texture. Fold the buttery cabbage through. Season generously.
👧 Kid-friendly trick: Before you fold the cabbage through the mash, scoop out a plain-mash portion for Child 3 (the 2-year-old) — plain buttery spud is often more popular than green-flecked spud at that age. Serve theirs on the side of a small pork-apple spoonful and add a few peas around it.
4
Peas on the go ~4 mins
Tip the peas into a small pot of boiling salted water. 3 minutes is enough. Drain, return to the hot pot, toss with 15g butter and a pinch of salt. Lid on.
5
Plate & serve ~2 mins
A generous scoop of colcannon on each plate, pressed into a nest with the back of the spoon. Spoon the pork & apple into the nest so the gravy pools in the mash. Buttery peas to the side. A final crack of pepper.
