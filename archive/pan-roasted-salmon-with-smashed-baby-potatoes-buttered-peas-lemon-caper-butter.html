🐟 Pan-Roasted Salmon with Smashed Baby Potatoes, Buttered Peas & Lemon-Caper Butter

~50 mins total
Serves 5
Salmon · Spring
Why this tonight? Week 2 Thursday in the rotation is open. Mon–Wed leaned beef and pork, so a fresh salmon supper is a lighter pivot. Pick up 5 salmon fillets from the fishmonger on the way home — Irish spring salmon is in good nick right now. Everything else is already in the press or freezer.

Ingredients

  • 5 salmon fillets, skin-on (~150–180g each)
  • 900g baby potatoes
  • 350g frozen peas
  • 60g butter (split)
  • 2 lemons
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp capers, drained
  • 3 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • Sea salt & black pepper
  • Small handful flat-leaf parsley (optional)

Prep Ahead

~25 min before pickup
Before you leave for pickup at 5:00 PM — get the potatoes par-boiled and smashed so the post-pickup window is just oven + pan-fry
Put a pan of well-salted water on for the baby potatoes and bring to the boil before
Boil the baby potatoes for 12–14 mins until just tender to a knife. Drain and let them dry off in the colander before
Tip onto an oiled tray, smash each one flat with the bottom of a glass, drizzle with rapeseed oil and a good pinch of salt. Set aside on the worktop, ready for the oven before
Mise en place: chop 2 cloves garlic, juice 1 lemon, slice the other into wedges, drain capers. Put it all on a small plate together before
Pat the salmon dry with kitchen paper, score the skin lightly (3 shallow slashes per fillet), salt the skin generously and leave fridge-side until you're back before
While you're at pickup
Smashed potatoes sit on the worktop drying out (good for crisping). Salmon sits salted, drawing moisture from the skin so it crisps up properly when it hits the pan.
⚠️ Before you leave: Hob off, oven off. Wash the board and knife you used for prepping — even though there's no raw meat, anything that touched raw fish gets a hot soapy rinse. Salted salmon goes back in the fridge if the kitchen's warm.

Method

1
Oven on, potatoes in ~2 mins active, 18 mins roast
Heat the oven to 220°C fan. Slide the tray of smashed baby potatoes in once it's up to temperature. Roast 18–20 mins until the edges are deep golden and crispy.
2
Pan on, salmon in ~6–8 mins
Heat your heaviest frying pan over medium-high. Add 2 tbsp rapeseed oil. When it shimmers, lay the salmon in skin-side down, away from you. Press each fillet flat for the first 30 seconds with a fish slice so the skin makes full contact. Cook undisturbed for 4–5 mins until the skin is crisp and the flesh is opaque about two-thirds of the way up.
💡 Probe it: Pop a wireless thermometer into the thickest fillet through the side. Pull at 55°C internal for a moist, just-cooked centre — it'll carry up to 58–60°C while resting. The toddlers will get the slimmer tail-end pieces, which cook through fully.
3
Flip and finish ~2–3 mins
Flip the fillets and cook 2–3 mins more until done (see probe note above). Lift onto a warm plate, skin-side up so it stays crisp. Tip the pan oil into the bin.
4
Build the lemon-caper butter ~2 mins
Lower the heat to medium. Drop 50g butter into the same pan. As it foams, add the chopped garlic and capers — sizzle for 30 seconds, no more. Squeeze in the juice of 1 lemon, swirl, and pull off the heat. Taste — it should be punchy and bright.
5
Buttered peas ~3 mins
Tip the frozen peas into a small saucepan with a splash of water and the remaining 10g butter. Cover and warm on medium for 3 mins, shaking the pan once or twice. Drain off any liquid, season with a pinch of salt.
6
Plate up ~2 mins
Pile the smashed potatoes on each plate, sit the salmon on top skin-side up, spoon a heap of buttered peas alongside. Drizzle the lemon-caper butter over the salmon and potatoes. Scatter parsley if using, lemon wedge on the side.
👧 Kid-friendly notes: Capers are bright and salty — for the 2- and 3-year-olds, plate their salmon and potatoes before drizzling the sauce, then give them a plain knob of butter on top. The 8-year-old can decide for himself. Crispy salmon skin is usually a hit with the wee ones once they try it; offer a piece on the side and don't push it.
Leftovers: Cooked salmon keeps 1–2 days fridge. Flake into pasta or onto toast with cream cheese for tomorrow's lunch. Don't reheat hard — gentle warmth or eat cold.
